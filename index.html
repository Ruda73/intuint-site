Same facts. How far apart is your exco?

Give every leader the same five operational scenarios, in tonnes, rands and months, and ask each one for an estimate alone. Before they start, each leader seals a guess of how much the team will disagree. In a published audit at an insurer, executives expected about 10% variation between underwriters pricing identical cases. They found 55%.

Judge → Measure → Grow

Every decision is a bet. Intuint keeps the score.

A decision is a prediction with something at stake. A prediction can be checked, and what can be checked can be scored and improved. Yet judgement is the one thing most organisations never measure.

01

Judge →

Before a decision, each leader commits to what they expect, twice: first from the gut, sealed when the decision is tabled and before the analysis is opened, then from the analysis. Both are locked in before anyone knows the outcome.

02

Measure →

When the outcome is in, every judgement is scored against reality: how often the gut was right, how often the analysis was right, and how well confidence matched accuracy.

03

Grow

Patterns appear that no single decision could show. Leaders learn where their intuition has earned trust and where it needs data beside it. Experience becomes a track record.

Gut × Feedback = Skill

Intuition only becomes skill when outcomes come back to it.

Confidence − Accuracy = Risk

The gap between how sure you are and how often you are right is where money is lost.

Opinion + Record = Evidence

A belief becomes evidence once you can see how often its owner has been right.

Built so it can't be gamed

A score is only worth having if it's honest

Locked before the outcomeEvery forecast is written to a tamper-evident ledger. Nobody can edit a prediction after the fact.
Gut first, sealedIntuition is captured before the analysis is opened, so the two stay genuinely independent.
Independent refereesSuccess is defined in advance, and outcomes are recorded by people who did not forecast.
Private by designEach person sees their own track record. The organisation sees patterns, never a league table.
